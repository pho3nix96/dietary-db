# Simulated 3-day food records (not real data)

Generated by `scripts/simulate_food_records.py --subjects 100 --days 3 --seed 42`
as test data for the co-intake graph and dietary-pattern code, in the FAO/WHO GIFT
microdata layout (subjects + consumption files, GIFT column names).

- 100 adults (18-74 y), 3 consecutive days from a random weekday, 6 GIFT eating occasions.
- Foods and nutrients from the Swedish Food Agency database (CC BY 4.0); each row has a real
  FoodEx2 code and GIFT group/subgroup.
- Latent dietary patterns (traditional Nordic, health-conscious, convenience, vegetarian),
  habitual favourite foods, weekend effects, portion noise and person-level under-reporting
  (mean factor 0.9). The truth is in `truth.csv`; hide it when testing methods.
- Unique products: 6% of food rows (225) are products that are NOT in the food table and are
  reported once (homemade, store-brand, unusual flavour), named by rewording a base food. 165 have
  label-style values per 100 g (the base food +/- ~15%), 60 have a name only. `in_database = False`
  marks them; `unique_foods_truth.csv` holds their hidden base food and true GIFT group.
- `placed_unique_foods.csv`: their placement in the food space (`scripts/place_unique_foods.py`);
  GIFT group recovered for 95% (name only) and 96% (name + values); 8 of 9 wrong groups were
  flagged as low confidence.

Replace with real records (e.g. INRAN-SCAI 2005-06, Italy: 3 consecutive-day food records
by meal, FoodEx2-coded, via FAO/WHO GIFT after login) when available.
